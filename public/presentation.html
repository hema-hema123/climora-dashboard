01 / Background
05 Introduction to Urban Microclimates
Microclimate Deterioration Dynamics
  • Urban morphology alters wind ventilation canyons and traps solar radiation.
  • Low-albedo construction materials (asphalt: 0.05-0.15) absorb up to 90% of solar flux.
  • Lack of vegetative evapotranspiration suppresses natural thermodynamic cooling.
  • Anthropogenic heat emissions from vehicular fleets and HVAC systems compound heat loads.
Socio-Economic & Ecological Impact
  • Public Health Hazards: Spike in heat strokes, cardiovascular fatigue, and nocturnal stress.
  • Vulnerability Skew: Slums and dense low-income zones bear the highest thermal burden.
  • Peak Energy Surges: Exponential air conditioning load strains regional electricity grids.
  • Ecological Degradation: Formation of ground-level tropospheric ozone and air stagnation.
02 / Physics
06 The Urban Heat Island Phenomenon
Day vs Night Heat Trapping Mechanics
  • Daytime Absorption: Dark pavements and concrete building facades act as giant thermal sponges, storing megajoules of solar radiation.
  • Nighttime Radiation Blockade: Tall narrow street canyons prevent infrared heat from radiating back into the upper atmosphere.
  • Nocturnal Heat Stress: Prevents human physiological thermal recovery, leading to dangerous heat-related chronic illnesses.
The Rural Baseline Contrast
  • Surrounding rural and forested zones experience high evapotranspiration from moist soil and lush foliage.
  • Rural areas cool rapidly after sunset, creating significant temperature deltas of +3°C to +7°C between the downtown center and countryside.
03 / Gap Analysis
07 Problem Statement & Industry Gaps
Limitations of Existing Weather Apps
  • Macro-Level Averaging: Reports a single city number (e.g. 'Bengaluru: 32°C'), masking 38°C+ hot zones.
  • Ground Station Scarcity: Physical weather stations are sparse, stationary, and costly to calibrate.
  • No Spatial Context: Fails to correlate local heat stress with road density or tree coverage.
  • Non-Actionable Data: Provides raw metrics without indicating where cooling projects will succeed.
The Climora Intervention
  • Continuous multi-spectral satellite surface temperature observations.
  • Automated spatial clustering identifying neighborhood thermal hazards.
  • Interactive 3D building extrusions illustrating height-to-heat relationships.
  • Prescriptive decision support with estimated temperature reduction and capital expenditure.

13 Mathematical Modeling & UHI Equations
1. UHI Thermal Differential Formula
ΔT_UHI = T_urban(lat, lon) - T_rural(lat_ref, lon_ref)
  • Dynamic Reference Sampling: Baseline evaluated ~50 km outside the municipal core.
  • Temperature Parameter: NASA POWER T2M (2-meter air temperature).
  • Relative Humidity Integration: Factored to compute ambient apparent heat index.
2. Severity Thresholds & Cooling ROI
  • Low Risk: ΔT < 1.0°C | Balanced microclimate
  • Moderate Risk: 1.0°C ≤ ΔT < 2.5°C | Mild vegetation loss
  • High Risk: 2.5°C ≤ ΔT < 4.0°C | Dense built infrastructure
  • Extreme Risk: ΔT ≥ 4.0°C | Severe thermal corridor

11 / Architecture
15 System Architecture & Specifications
⚡ Client-First Serverless Paradigm

Climora runs 100% serverless on Vercel Edge. By avoiding heavy Express and database overhead, the client orchestrates data directly with high resilience.

  • Direct Satellite Telemetry: Client-side queries to NASA POWER point APIs.
  • Zero Database Overhead: Deterministic spatial seeding ensures ₹0 maintenance costs.
  • Intelligent Query Caching: TanStack React Query deduplicates identical coordinate requests.
🏗️ Layered Implementation Details
  • Presentation: Next.js 16 (App Router), React 19, Tailwind CSS 4, Radix UI Primitives.
  • GIS Rendering: Mapbox GL with vector tiles and client-side hardware-accelerated WebGL.
  • State Synchronization: Zustand global store binds time slider, selected city, and heat layers.
  • Analytics Visualization: Responsive AreaChart and BarChart components built with Recharts.
